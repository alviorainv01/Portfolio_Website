Pulseboard

Real-time product analytics that early-stage teams actually open every morning — 40k events a minute in, sub-second answers out.

RoleFounder & lead engineer TimelineJan 2025 → present TeamMe + 1 designer (contract) StatusLive, seed-funded

01 · The problemAnalytics shouldn't feel like archaeology

Every founder I talked to had the same complaint: existing tools either batched data overnight or priced them out entirely. Teams were making launch decisions with yesterday's numbers, and switching costs pushed them to tolerate it.

The bet for Pulseboard: if dashboards were genuinely live and p95 latency stayed under 200 ms, small SaaS teams would pay for it daily — not as infrastructure, but as a habit.

02 · ArchitectureOne pipeline, boring on purpose

  • Ingest: edge collectors validate and batch events, then hand off to a Kafka buffer so bursts never hit the store directly.
  • Store: ClickHouse with materialised views pre-aggregating the ten queries teams run 90% of the time.
  • Serve: a websocket fan-out layer groups viewers by query signature — 200 concurrent dashboards share one query, not two hundred.
  • App: Next.js client with optimistic filters; Postgres holds accounts and billing, Stripe handles subscriptions.

The least glamorous decision paid off most: shadow reads before every migration. Nine services moved to new schemas with zero downtime, because old and new paths ran side-by-side against production traffic until results matched byte-for-byte.

03 · Hardest problemFan-out without melting

A single customer launched their dashboard to a 200-person all-hands. Each browser opened its own websocket and issued its own query — ClickHouse saw 200 identical scans at once and fell over. The fix was a query-signature registry: identical live queries dedupe into one subscription, results broadcast to all sockets. Peak database load dropped roughly 97% for busy rooms, and the incident became the product's load-test suite.

"It's the only analytics tool our whole team has open during standup." — design partner, week 9

04 · Outcome & lessonsGrowth as an engineer of one

  • 60 paying teams within six months of launch, from a waitlist seeded by a build-in-public thread.
  • Churn under 3%/month — live alerts turned out to be the stickiest feature, not the dashboards.
  • Lesson learned: sell the workflow, not the warehouse. Demos that started with schema talk lost rooms; demos that started with "what did you ship yesterday?" closed them.
  • Next: cohort explorer and a public API — both shaped directly by support transcripts.
