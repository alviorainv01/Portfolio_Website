Waypoint

A trip planner that keeps working where the signal doesn't — shared itineraries, offline maps, one tiny PWA.

RoleSolo side project TimelineJun 2023 → ongoing Growth0 → 12k monthly users StatusLive, community-driven

01 · The problemDay two in the Himalayas breaks most travel apps

The pattern I kept seeing on group trips: one person plans everything in a doc, everyone else improvises from screenshots, and map apps die the moment the road runs out of towers. Existing planners were either collaborative but cloud-bound, or offline but single-player.

Waypoint's promise combined both: collaborative planning that degrades gracefully to zero bars — and installs from a URL, no app store, so it works on any phone a traveller carries.

02 · How it worksPrecache the route, not the whole world

  • Corridor caching: when an itinerary is saved, Waypoint computes a buffer around each day's route and downloads only those Mapbox vector tiles — typically 18 MB for a week in Himachal, versus gigabytes for region downloads.
  • Collaboration: Supabase Realtime row subscriptions drive presence cursors and item-level edits, so four friends can shuffle the same day without stomping on each other.
  • Size discipline: Svelte's compile-time reactivity keeps the whole client under 120 KB gzipped; the service worker, cache eviction and UI all fit inside it.
  • Install anywhere: one codebase ships as PWA on iOS, Android and desktop — including the add-to-homescreen flow Apple makes deliberately awkward.

03 · Hardest problemCache policy when travellers are irrational

Users build a trip, then change it completely three days before flying — after their last reliable wifi. Naive caching either wasted mobile data on routes never flown or evicted tiles mid-trek. The solution was a confidence score per itinerary segment: booked stays and flights pin tiles permanently, speculative days get soft-evicted, and the app nags with a one-tap "final sync" whenever strong wifi appears before departure.

"It was the only app that worked in Pin Valley." — user review after a Spiti circuit trip

04 · Outcome & lessonsFrom side project to commons

  • 12k monthly users; hit #4 on Hacker News and r/travel within the first year.
  • 1,900 GitHub stars and 40 community route packs (Ladakh, Alps, Rajasthan) that act as ready-made templates for new users.
  • Lesson learned: PWAs are judged by their worst platform. Budget an extra month purely for iOS quirks — storage quotas especially.
  • Next: GPX import/export and a shareable "live on trip" view for families back home.
