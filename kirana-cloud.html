Kirana Cloud

Inventory and billing for neighbourhood stores — built to keep working when the network doesn't.

RoleSolo builder TimelineMar 2024 → Dec 2025 LicenseOpen source, MIT Status~900 weekly shops

01 · The problemThe counter can't wait for the cloud

A kirana shop runs on a ₹6,000 Android phone, patchy 4G and one overloaded charger. Every SaaS billing tool I trialled assumed the opposite: constant connectivity, desktop-class hardware, English-first forms. When the network dropped mid-sale, the whole till stopped.

The design constraint followed directly: the app must be fully functional offline, sync without conflicts when a staff member's phone reconnects days later, and print a GST invoice in seconds on the cheapest Bluetooth printers on the market.

02 · How it worksCRDTs where others use retry queues

  • Local-first data: every device holds a full replica of the shop's stock ledger as a CRDT; edits merge deterministically, so two clerks selling the last packet of dal never double-book it or lose either sale.
  • Sync: a thin Node.js/PostgreSQL relay stores op logs and fans them out on reconnect — no server round-trip is ever on the critical path of a sale.
  • Scanning: the camera pipeline recognises Indian barcodes and vernacular labels offline; unknown items fall back to a two-tap search that learns from the shop's own history.
  • Printing: ESC/POS output tuned for the sub-₹1,500 thermal printers shops actually own — a receipt prints in under 3 seconds.

Battery was a first-class metric: an 8-hour shift drains under 2%, verified nightly on a rack of five budget devices via GitHub Actions + Playwright.

03 · Hardest problemDesigning for people who won't read a manual

Early pilots failed quietly: shopkeepers never reported bugs, they just went back to notebooks. The fix was observational — I spent three Saturdays behind counters in Pune. That produced the two features that turned adoption: a voice-speed undo (shake the phone to reverse the last line item) and a daily WhatsApp summary card that shows sales and low stock without opening the app.

"Pehle register tha, ab phone hai — dono same speed." (Notebook or phone, same speed.) — pilot shop, Kothrud, Pune

04 · Outcome & lessonsOpen source as an acquisition channel

  • ~900 weekly shops across Pune and Bengaluru; growth driven by shop-owner word of mouth, not marketing.
  • MIT licensing paid off twice: a college student contributed the Kannada localisation, and a payments startup now funds the sync relay's hosting.
  • Lesson learned: in local-first apps, conflict UX matters more than conflict resolution — merges are automatic, but showing *what* merged is what earns trust.
  • Next: UPI payment-request links baked into invoices, and a wholesale-supplier reorder view.
